### The one differing number, explained

adj3_r3_1.py's Part 3 built its widened balls from the γ-run
payload's **printed** ball strings: lo = printed_mid −
printed_rad, hi = printed_mid + printed_rad + D_k. In
python-flint's display convention the printed radius exceeds the
internal radius (it also covers the printed midpoint's
truncation); for the moments J^{12,20} consumes (k = 20..32) the
printed/internal radius ratios in the record are 1.67–9.31. The
replay instead recomputes the internal balls directly (the
recorded float representations of their radii agree with the
record's internal radii for all 33 indices, above), so its
coefficient balls for the high moments are narrower, and the
discriminant ball of γ J^{12,20} — whose width is governed by
those moments, the widening D_k being negligible at k ≥ 20 —
comes out narrower: 1.495401392981139e-11 against the
certificate's 2.612920159759354e-11. Both are valid enclosures of
the same discriminant; every status agrees. The certificate of
record's figure (2.61e-11) remains the conservative number of
record for `results_adj3.json`; the replay's figure is this
replay's own. A replay whose worst width *exceeded* the
certificate's, or whose worst polynomial differed, would be a
reportable deviation under the README's comparison contract —
a regression-check finding, not by itself a failure of the
certification outcome (the contract separates the two).

bytes, sha256 1e77032b…), run end to end on the full grid,

`SCHEMA-CROSSWALK.md` in this package.
